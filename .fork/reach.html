A Phone Anywhere

The console is built and a phone has walked it. What was missing on 2026-09-08 was reach: the phone could pair only on the home LAN. This page is the choice made that day, the one being built toward, what was measured in between, and the ops you own before tomorrow evening.

where it stands, 2026-09-08 evening

Tailscale is installed on the PC and on the Android phone, both logged into one free account. The PC is 100.82.213.46, the phone 100.100.201.63. The launcher (warpdev.ps1 -Console) now binds the console to the PC's tailnet address by default, resolved from tailscale ip -4 at launch, so the phone's saved URL is https://100.82.213.46:41234/ from anywhere it has signal. The certificate is minted per launch for whichever address is bound and signed by the authority the phone already installed, so nothing on the phone changes.

One thing the measurement found that the manual had predicted only in outline: with ProtonVPN connected, every packet between the two devices goes through a Tailscale relay in Chicago. See the snag below. It works; it is slower and it is their box.

What "remote" has to mean here

Three properties, and every option below is judged on them. The words stay off third-party machines: prompts, approvals, the trace. The console stays a secure context on the phone, because notifications and the home-screen install depend on it, and that means https:// to an address the phone's browser trusts. And nothing is opened inbound on the home router, because the manual measured on 2026-09-02 that a port-forward to this PC breaks the moment ProtonVPN connects (the reply leaves through the tunnel and the router never sees it).

The fork already has one layer nobody can take away: the console speaks TLS with a certificate Warp minted and the phone installed (tls.rs, 2026-09-06). So whatever carries the packets, the relay sees TLS to Warp's own authority, never the words. The network layer's job is reach and a second envelope, not the only envelope.

The options

Tailscale, their coordination server TODAY, STOP-GAP

Mesh WireGuard between your own devices. Tailscale's server sees each device's public key, name, IP endpoints and which account they belong to; it never holds a session key and cannot read traffic. When two devices cannot reach each other directly, their DERP relays carry the encrypted packets and see size and timing. The account is the off-thesis part: a login with a third party, held for as long as the tailnet exists.

What it costs you: nothing, and twenty minutes. What it buys: the phone at work reaches the console, SSH and everything else on the PC at one stable address, with no inbound port and no DNS name. Switching to headscale later is one command per device, and the account is deleted afterwards.

headscale on a VPS THE DESTINATION

The same clients, the same protocol, your own coordination server. What Tailscale's server saw above is now seen by a box you rent. With derper on the same box, the relay is yours too, and the only third parties left are the VPS host (sees an encrypted WireGuard stream and a metadata service) and the DNS host (sees that a name exists).

Requirements are small: one vCPU, 1 GB, Debian or Ubuntu, a public IPv4, a DNS name pointing at it. Ports 443/tcp for headscale, 3478/udp and one more tcp port if you run the relay. An evening of ops; the steps are below.

Cloudflare Tunnel REFUSED

You asked whether this was the right shape. It is the wrong one for this fork, and the reason is a single fact: Cloudflare terminates TLS at its edge. cloudflared on the PC makes an outbound connection to Cloudflare; the phone connects to a public hostname on Cloudflare; Cloudflare decrypts the phone's request, reads it, and forwards it to the PC. Every prompt, every approval, the whole trace passes through their machines in the clear. That is a relay that reads the words, which is the one thing the phone surface was designed not to have (mobile.html, "where the words go: nowhere").

It also needs a Cloudflare account, a domain on their DNS, and it gives the console a public name, which the spec refused separately. Cloudflare's private-network mode (their WARP client plus a tunnel) is the same architecture with the client on the phone: WireGuard from the phone to their edge, decrypted there, re-encrypted to the PC. Not end to end. There is no writeup of it in the repo because it was never a candidate; this paragraph is that writeup.

Plain WireGuard hub on the VPS VIABLE, SECOND CHOICE

No headscale: a WireGuard server on the VPS, the PC and the phone as peers, all traffic hairpinning through it. Simpler to set up than headscale and it has no coordination server at all. What you give up is direct paths (every packet goes through the box, always), NAT traversal, and the phone app's one-toggle experience; you also maintain peer configs by hand. Worth knowing as the fallback if headscale ever annoys you; not worth choosing first.

Port-forward, Tailscale Funnel, a public certificate RULED OUT

A port-forward to the PC dies when ProtonVPN connects (measured 2026-09-02). Funnel publishes the console to the internet under a Tailscale-owned name, through their proxy. A public certificate needs a public name for a console meant for one phone. All three were already refused in mobile.html or manual.md; listed here so the list is complete.

The snag, measured

With ProtonVPN connected, tailscale netcheck on the PC reported its public endpoint as a Proton exit (185.98.171.45) and MappingVariesByDestIP: true, which is the NAT shape that defeats hole punching. tailscale ping to the phone answered four times via DERP(ord) at 250 to 480 ms and then direct connection not established. So the tunnel under the tunnel works, and it works by relaying every packet through Tailscale's Chicago relay.

Measured the same evening with ProtonVPN disconnected (tailscale-without-protonvpn.txt): the public endpoint was the home WAN, MappingVariesByDestIP: false, and both phones went direct, the Android over cellular through its carrier's NAT at 276 ms and the iPhone on the LAN at 5 ms. So the line supports direct paths and ProtonVPN is the only reason for the relay.

Three ways out, in the order to try them:

  1. Take tailscaled.exe out of ProtonVPN: tried, and it does not work. Proton's exclude-mode split tunneling was applied to C:\Program Files\Tailscale\tailscaled.exe on 2026-09-08 and the daemon lost every path out: all connection attempts failed ... A socket operation was attempted to an unreachable network, the node showed offline in the admin console, and a session restart did not help. Whatever Proton does for an excluded user app, it did not give a SYSTEM service a working route while the tunnel was up. Removed again. If Proton's split tunneling ever gains a per-service or per-address form that works, this is the cheapest fix; until then it is not one.

  2. Run your own relay. Once headscale is on the VPS, derper beside it makes the relayed path yours. Latency stays whatever the VPS's location gives; ownership changes.

  3. Accept it. Everything works today over the relay; a permission answer takes half a second longer. The words are not exposed. This is the state the stop-gap is in as this page is written.

What to do before tomorrow evening

All of this is yours to own; none of it is code in the fork. When it is done, the switch on each device is one step and the fork needs no change, because WARP_FORK_CONTROL_BIND takes a tailnet address today and does not care who coordinates it.

1. Pick a provider

The box does almost nothing: a few hundred MB of memory, negligible traffic unless the relay is used, no data at rest but device keys. So the choice is about jurisdiction, what they ask of you at signup, and how they pay. Read, not used; verify the current terms before paying.

2. Stand up headscale

On the VPS, as root, Debian 12 or Ubuntu 24.04. This is the manual's recipe with the relay added and the TLS choice made: headscale's built-in Let's Encrypt, so no reverse proxy.

# the control server
curl -fsSL -o headscale.deb \
  https://github.com/juanfont/headscale/releases/latest/download/headscale_linux_amd64.deb
apt install ./headscale.deb

# /etc/headscale/config.yaml, the lines that matter
#   server_url: https://hs.example.com
#   listen_addr: 0.0.0.0:443
#   tls_letsencrypt_hostname: hs.example.com
#   tls_letsencrypt_listen: ":80"
#   derp:
#     urls: []                      # drop Tailscale's public relay map
#     paths: [/etc/headscale/derp.yaml]
systemctl enable --now headscale
headscale users create me
headscale preauthkeys create --user me --reusable --expiration 24h

Then the relay on the same box, on ports that do not collide with 443:

# the relay: tailscale's derper, built from source or from a release
go install tailscale.com/cmd/derper@latest
# run it as a service on, say, 3340/tcp with STUN on 3478/udp, with its own
# Let's Encrypt name (derp.example.com) or the same one on a different port
derper -hostname derp.example.com -a :3340 -stun-port 3478 -verify-clients

# /etc/headscale/derp.yaml
regions:
  900:
    regionid: 900
    regioncode: home
    regionname: mine
    nodes:
      - name: 900a
        regionid: 900
        hostname: derp.example.com
        derpport: 3340
        stunport: 3478

Firewall on the VPS: 22 (your key only), 80 and 443 tcp, 3340 tcp, 3478 udp; ufw and unattended-upgrades before anything else. -verify-clients on the relay makes it refuse anyone not on your tailnet, and it needs a local tailscaled on the VPS logged into the same headscale to do that check; leave it off for the first test and turn it on once the box itself is a node.

3. Move the devices

# the PC (PowerShell)
& 'C:\Program Files\Tailscale\tailscale.exe' logout
& 'C:\Program Files\Tailscale\tailscale.exe' up --login-server https://hs.example.com --authkey <key>
& 'C:\Program Files\Tailscale\tailscale.exe' ip -4      # a new 100.x address: the phone's saved URL changes once

The phone: the official app, logged out, then the settings menu in the upper right, custom control server, https://hs.example.com; it opens a page with a headscale nodes register line to run on the VPS. On iOS the same, under the top-right menu as "use custom coordination server". Both from headscale's own docs, read 2026-09-08, not yet run here.

Then delete the Tailscale account from its admin console. Nothing on either device refers to it after the switch.

4. Prove it

  • tailscale status on the PC lists the phone and the VPS; tailscale ping to the phone says direct, or via DERP(home) if the relay is in use and it is yours.
  • ProtonVPN connected, then the same two commands again. The manual's warning about the kill switch stands: if one is ever armed permanently, test this before relying on it from a train.
  • The phone on cellular, Wi-Fi off: https://<new address>:41234/ loads with no warning, a scanned code pairs, an approval lands via paired_device.

A working day, as it is meant to go

  1. Morning, at the desk. warpdev.ps1 -Console (or ggwarpdev console). Start the conversation in a pane, cd it where the work is, click /remote-control, scan the block with the phone. The block says paired at HH:MM. Add the page to the home screen if it is not there. Leave.

  2. At work. Tailscale on the phone connected (it stays connected; it costs little). Found 2026-09-08 on the Android phone, on cellular: with Tailscale on, Android reported Private DNS server cannot be accessed and treated the whole network as down. That is Android's Private DNS (DNS over TLS to a named server) failing to reach its server through the tunnel; the console does not need DNS at all, which is why the pairing still worked. Fix on the phone: Settings, Network & internet, Private DNS, set to Off or Automatic; or in the Tailscale app turn off "Use Tailscale DNS". The tailnet pushes no resolvers (tailscale dns status), only the MagicDNS suffix, so nothing is lost. Off fixed it the same evening.

    The iPhone, the same evening, Wi-Fi only: it joined the tailnet, dropped off while being set up (reconnect the app's switch), then installed the authority. Safari on /ca.crt offers a profile; Settings shows Profile Downloaded near the top, else General, VPN & Device Management; install; then General, About, Certificate Trust Settings, full trust. The install looks finished before that last step and Safari still calls the console insecure until it is done. Open the icon. The page reconnects to the stream, the header says the mode, model and directory. Firefox on Android delivers the buzz to the lock screen when the agent asks (measured 2026-09-07); Chrome only while the page is foreground. Answer, prompt, read the trace.

  3. What ends it. Stop sharing at the desk, the conversation being deleted, or Warp closing. Not a clock: the control pairing has none since 2026-09-06. The five-minute credentials turn over on their own.

  4. What it cannot do, by decision. Start a new conversation; change the agent's permission mode; answer for any other conversation. If the agent's own login expires (it did overnight on 2026-09-07), the phone sees a failed turn with the agent's error text, and the remedy is /login at the desk. Plan the day around one conversation, started before leaving.

  5. The backstop. SSH from Termux over the same address reaches all of warpctrl, tmux and the agent itself. The Windows rule for port 22 is scoped to the LAN subnet today and needs 100.64.0.0/10 added; one elevated command, recorded in the run when it is done.

what the stop-gap costs, stated once

Until the switch, Tailscale Inc. holds an account in your name with the public keys and names of two devices, and relays their encrypted packets whenever ProtonVPN is up. It never holds a session key and cannot read anything. That is the whole disclosure, and it is the reason headscale is the destination rather than a preference.
